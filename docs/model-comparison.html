Deep Learning project · de Castro Moraes et al. (2024) on Rossmann

LSTM vs S-CNN-LSTM vs P-CNN-LSTM

All three models get the same input and predict the same thing: next-day sales for all 934 stores at once. They differ in what the convolutional block looks at and how the LSTM and CNN are connected.

7 days × 956 values

One input sample is a window of the 7 previous days, shape (7, 956). Each day holds 934 store sales (scaled to 0–1, grey) followed by 22 features (orange): 7 day-of-week flags, 12 month flags, and the share of stores on a state holiday, school holiday and promo.

The output is a vector of 934 numbers: tomorrow's scaled sales for every store, shape (934,). Loss MSE, Adam with learning rate 0.001, batch 32, early stopping on validation MAE.

Why the first Conv1D differs: 38,528 vs 367,232 parameters

A Conv1D weight count is kernel × input channels × filters + filters. In S-CNN-LSTM the first convolution reads the LSTM's 100 channels: 3 × 100 × 128 + 128 = 38,528. In P-CNN-LSTM it reads the raw 956 channels: 3 × 956 × 128 + 128 = 367,232. This one number shows the whole difference: the stacked CNN sees what the LSTM remembered, while the parallel CNN sees the raw sales and calendar.

What happens to the 7 days inside the CNN block

Convolutions use padding="same", so they keep the length. Each average pooling divides it (rounding up). The paper's pool sizes (2, 7, 10) were set for its data; with our 7-day window the time axis collapses to a single step after the second pooling.

Conv1D 128 → 7 steps
AvgPool 2 → 4 steps
AvgPool 7 → 1 step
AvgPool 10 → 1 step

So the third convolution (k=3) works on one time step: in practice it acts as a 256 → 500 fully connected layer on the summary of the whole week.

Hybrids beat the LSTM on Rossmann

Both hybrids are 0.15–0.66 MAPE points better than the LSTM baseline, the same direction as the paper.

Stacked vs parallel is a tie

The gaps between S and P are smaller than their spread across seeds. The paper found parallel generally better; on our data neither wins clearly.

Parallel is the most stable

P-CNN-LSTM's seed-to-seed spread is smaller than S-CNN-LSTM's on every row (1.5 to 7 times), which fits the paper's ensemble argument.
